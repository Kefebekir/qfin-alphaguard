PRE-REGISTRATION N3
Written 2026-10-18 17:07 UTC; approved by Efe before any result of this test was computed,
and before N2's holdout runs.

Does a larger point-in-time language model read members' news better? N2, with a
4-billion-parameter model in place of ChronoBERT.

HYPOTHESIS
As N2: among S&P 500 members with news since the last close, those whose headlines lead a
model, trained only on earlier years, to forecast a better next day earn more from today's
close to tomorrow's close than those it forecasts worst. And: a larger model reads them
better. Among point-in-time models, the 4-billion-parameter PIT-4B did best on next-day
returns from headlines in DatedGPT's comparison (Yan et al., 2026: Sharpe 3.37 for PIT-4B,
3.20 for DatedGPT, 0.86 for PIT-4B-FT, 0.59 for ChronoGPT-Instruct), and Kelly, Malamud,
Schwab and Xu (NeurIPS 2026) find its news embeddings, fine-tuned or not, ahead of
ChronoGPT's on monthly returns (Sharpe 1.53 and 1.02 against 0.67).

DATA
As N2 (the articles, the window from 16:00 on the session before to 15:30, the members, the
returns against the mean of members with news), with N2's costs after amendment 2: 1 bp a
round trip on each side.

FEATURES (fixed here)
- The models: PIT-4B, base and fine-tuned (PIT-4B-FT), Apache-2.0, published under
  huggingface.co/Diamegs and huggingface.co/anonymneurips2027 with the same files; each
  checkpoint is pinned by the SHA-256 of its model.safetensors, written in the ledger before
  it is used. Year Y's headlines are read by the checkpoint of December Y-1; 2016's by
  November 2015 (201511), the earliest before it; 2025's and 2026's by December 2024
  (202412), the latest published. Each checkpoint's month is the one its config.json states.
- A headline's two embeddings, from one pass through each model: the last layer's hidden
  state after the final RMS norm at the headline's last token (Kelly et al.'s choice), and
  the mean of that state over the headline's tokens. Computed in bfloat16 by the project's
  own copy of the published architecture, so no code from the models' repositories runs.
- A member-day's features: the mean of its window's headline embeddings, and log(1 + their
  number), as N2.
- Four feature sets follow (base or fine-tuned, last token or mean). Which one a variant
  uses is part of the variant.

MODEL (trained by Efe; the protocol fixed here)
- Target, family and policy as N2: principal components and ridge, at most 10 variants on
  development in all, across the four feature sets, each in the ledger before the next is
  run. A variant may add, as inputs, the member's return that day, its 20-day volatility and
  its momentum from 12 months to 1 month before.
- The variant with the highest mean a day on the development years goes to the holdout,
  whatever it is.

SPLITS
- Development: test years 2018-2021, fitted on the years from 2016 before each.
- Holdout: 2022 to 9 October 2026, each year fitted on all years before it, run once after
  this file and the chosen variant are committed.

TEST
- The long-short's mean daily return after costs over the holdout: one-sided t >= 2.50.
  N1 used half of the news tests' 2.5%; N2 and N3 share the other half (Bonferroni, 0.625%
  each), so N2's threshold rises to the same 2.50 (amendment 3).
- Reported, not part of the pass:
  - before costs;
  - the long side and the short side apart;
  - each year;
  - N3 less N2, day by day, with its t.
- If it passes, the model is frozen on a card and judged forward in the shadow record; if it
  fails, it is recorded as failed.
